## Candidates (catalog-row shape, ready to paste)

### 1. ceyssens-2019 — knees / feet_strike / hips_pelvis
- Title: Biomechanical risk factors associated with running-related injuries: a systematic review
- Authors: [Ceyssens L et al.] — Journal: Sports Medicine — Year: 2019 (49(7):1095-1115)
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31028658/ — DOI: 10.1007/s40279-019-01110-z
- Key finding: Systematic review synthesizing which running mechanics predict running-related injury across body regions — the cross-zone evidence map behind scoring every zone separately instead of one global score. (Already cited in the site FAQ "THE SCIENCE" answer.)
- Verified: multi-source citation strings agree on journal/pages/DOI/PMID 31028658.

### 2. cavanagh-williams-1982 — knees / feet_strike
- Title: The effect of stride length variation on oxygen uptake during distance running
- Authors: [Cavanagh PR, Williams KR] — Journal: Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise — Year: 1982 (14(1):30-35)
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7070254/ — DOI: 10.1249/00005768-198201000-00006
- Key finding: Recreational runners do not spontaneously choose their most economical stride length, and imposed stride changes alter oxygen cost — the historical basis for cadence/stride length as a trainable variable behind the knees-zone cadence cue.
- Verified: PubMed record (PMID 7070254) + consistent DOI across citing sources.

### 3. robbins-hanna-1987 — feet_strike
- Title: Running-related injury prevention through barefoot adaptations
- Authors: [Robbins SE et al.] — Journal: Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise — Year: 1987 (19(2):148-156)
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2883551/ — DOI: 10.1249/00005768-198704000-00014
- Key finding: Barefoot runners adapt footfall behavior to moderate impact via proprioceptive feedback that cushioned shoes dull — early basis for scoring foot strike + overstride behavior rather than footwear. (Follow-up Robbins & Gouw 1989: innate impact-moderating behavior.)
- Verified: PMID 2883551 + DOI consistent across medical/sports sources.

### 4. williams-cavanagh-1987 — knees / feet_strike
- Title: Relationship between distance running mechanics, running economy, and performance
- Authors: [Williams KR, Cavanagh PR] — Journal: Journal of Applied Physiology — Year: 1987 (63:1236-1245)
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3654469/ — DOI: 10.1152/jappl.1987.63.3.1236
- Key finding: Mapped which measurable running mechanics relate to economy and performance — the basis for scoring movement quality (not just injury risk) in the knees/feet_strike zones.
- Verified: PubMed record (PMID 3654469) + DOI consistent across citing papers.

### 5. bramble-lieberman-2004 — head_neck / trunk_lean
- Title: Endurance running and the evolution of Homo
- Authors: [Bramble DM, Lieberman DE] — Journal: Nature — Year: 2004 (432(7015):345-352)
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15549097/ — DOI: 10.1038/nature03052
- Key finding: Humans evolved as endurance runners ~2M years ago with running-specific adaptations of head stabilization, trunk counter-rotation and leg spring — the deep basis for scoring head, trunk and legs as one system. First dedicated head_neck backing beyond Souza.
- Verified: PubMed record (PMID 15549097) + DOI cross-confirmed.

### 6. pontzer-2009 — arms_elbows / shoulders / head_neck
- Title: Control and function of arm swing in human walking and running
- Authors: [Pontzer H et al.] — Journal: Journal of Experimental Biology — Year: 2009 (212:523-534)
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19181900/ — DOI: 10.1242/jeb.024927
- Key finding: Arms act as passive mass dampers powered by lower-body movement (deltoids co-contract rather than drive), with trunk/shoulders as elastic linkages that reduce torso and head rotation — the mechanistic basis for the arms_elbows and shoulders zones. First dedicated arms backing.
- Verified: Duke/author-tree records agree (PMID 19181900, DOI); hypothesis + deltoid result quoted in academic sources.

### 7. noehren-2007 — hips_pelvis / knees
- Title: ASB clinical biomechanics award winner 2006 — Prospective study of the biomechanical factors associated with iliotibial band syndrome
- Authors: [Noehren B, Davis I, Hamill J] — Journal: Clinical Biomechanics — Year: 2007 (22(9):951-956)
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17728030/ — DOI: 10.1016/j.clinbiomech.2007.07.001
- Key finding: Prospective design — runners who later developed ITBS already showed greater peak hip adduction and knee internal rotation than controls who stayed healthy, proving hip/pelvis mechanics predict injury rather than result from it. Backs strict hips_pelvis scoring.
- Verified: PMID 17728030 + DOI consistent; directional result confirmed via JOSPT review, IJSPT commentary and Sports Med 2021 review.

### 8. crowell-davis-2011 — feet_strike / knees
- Title: Gait retraining to reduce lower extremity loading in runners
- Authors: [Crowell HP, Davis IS] — Journal: Clinical Biomechanics (NOT JOSPT — corrected) — Year: 2011 (26(1):78-83)
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20888675/ — DOI: 10.1016/j.clinbiomech.2010.09.003
- Key finding: Runners given real-time visual feedback on impact loading successfully retrained to softer landings — proof the feet_strike/knees flaws we flag are fixable, backing drill prescription (not just diagnosis).
- Verified: PMID 20888675 + DOI consistent across PLOS/PMC citing sources.

### 9. lenhart-2014 — knees
- Title: Increasing running step rate reduces patellofemoral joint forces
- Authors: [Lenhart RL et al.] — Journal: Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise — Year: 2014 (46(3):557-564)
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23917470/ — DOI: 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3182a78c3a (PMCID PMC3925193)
- Key finding: Raising step rate cuts patellofemoral joint forces, with reduced peak stance-phase knee flexion as the key predictor — the knee-specific mechanism behind the +5-10% cadence cue, complementing Heiderscheit 2011.
- Verified: PubMed record (PMID 23917470) + mechanism detail via science.gov topic page.

### 10. lang-2023 — shoulders / hips_pelvis
- Title: Relationship between longitudinal upper body rotation and energy cost of running in junior elite long-distance runners
- Authors: [Lang C et al.] — Journal: Sports — Year: 2023 (11(10):204)
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37888531/ — DOI: 10.3390/sports11100204 (PMCID PMC10611096, open access)
- Key finding: Freer horizontal shoulder/pelvis rotation slightly lowers energy cost (sex-specific: shoulder rotation for females, pelvis for males) — backs scoring rigid, locked shoulders/hips as an efficiency flaw.
- Verified: PMID 37888531, DOI and author list via MDPI/DOAJ/Mendeley records; sex-specific correlation detail via PMC full text.
