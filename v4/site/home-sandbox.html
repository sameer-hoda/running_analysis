Running form questions, answered

Grouped accordion treatment of the live FAQ. Full answers live on the homepage FAQ.

THE BASICS

What is running form?

How your body moves when you run — posture, foot strike, knee drive, arm swing and lean. Small flaws raise injury risk and waste energy.

Can it really be analyzed from a single photo?

Yes for visible mechanics — foot strike, overstride, lean, knee drive, arm swing, head and hip alignment. What a photo cannot show is never guessed.

FIX YOUR ZONES

How do I stop overstriding?

Land with the foot under the hips instead of reaching ahead: lift cadence slightly, run quieter, stop reaching.

Is heel striking bad?

Not by itself. The red flag is heel strike plus overstride, a locked knee and low cadence, which together create braking force.

SCORES

What is a good running form score?

Most recreational runners score 55–75 out of 100. Anything under 60 usually means two or more zones need drills.

How is my score calculated?

Each visible zone scores 0–100 and the overall is the rounded average. Any critical zone caps the total at 70, two or more cap it at 60.

THE SCIENCE

What research is this based on?

Systematic reviews on running-injury biomechanics, the evidence-based video-analysis framework, cadence-loading studies, and foot-strike work. All linked below.

What can’t a photo measure?

Cadence, ground-contact time, vertical bounce, loading rate, leg stiffness and pronation speed — so they are never guessed from your photo.
